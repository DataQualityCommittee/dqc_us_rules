# Calculated Ratio Consistency Checks
Rule ID: DQC.US.0227.10920  
Status: Public Review  
Public Review Release date: TBD  
Effective date: TBD

## Rule Function

**Purpose:**

The purpose of Rule DQC.US.0227.10920 is to verify that reported calculated ratio concepts (for example, percentages and per-share metrics) are consistent with their underlying numerator and denominator facts. The rule validates that the reported calculated fact lies within the numeric interval implied by dividing the numerator by the denominator, taking decimals and tolerance for rounding into account.

**Conditions:**

The rule applies to the configured ratio concepts in the `$NUM_DEN_FULL` mapping when the filing form is applicable to the rule.
- For each configured ratio concept, the rule locates the reported calculated fact (the ratio), the numerator facts, and the denominator facts with compatible units and non-zero values.
- The rule computes the numeric interval implied by dividing the numerator by the denominator and compares that with the reported fact's interval.
- For concepts with datatype `dtr-types:perShareItemType`, the rule applies a tolerance of **0.01** (one cent per share) to the interval bounds before comparing, so that per-share rounding differences do not trigger false positives.
- `EarningsPerShareBasic` is not evaluated for short reporting periods: fewer than 310 days for `FY`, fewer than 120 days for `Q2`, or fewer than 200 days for `Q3`.
- `EarningsPerShareBasic` is also not evaluated when the filing uses the two-class method, indicated by the presence of a non-nil two-class-method earnings-per-share concept.
- If the calculated interval and the reported fact's interval do not overlap after applying any applicable tolerance, the rule triggers an error indicating an inconsistency.

## Problem Solved by the Rule

This rule catches cases where a ratio or percentage value reported in the taxonomy does not match the arithmetic of its components (numerator / denominator). Discrepancies can arise from incorrect tagging, improper decimal precision, or transcription/calculation errors. Ensuring the reported ratio is consistent with its components increases confidence in numeric accuracy and helps identify issues early in automated validation.

## Technical Details

The rule implementation follows these steps:
1. Use the configured mapping (`$NUM_DEN_FULL`) of ratio concept -> [numeratorConcept, denominatorConcept]. The mapping is maintained in the rule as an interim representation of the numerator and denominator relationships from the meta taxonomy.
2. If the filing form is not applicable to the rule, skip the rule. Otherwise, for each configured ratio concept key:
   - Locate the reported ratio fact (`$calc`) for the ratio concept.
   - Locate the numerical numerator facts (`$numerator`) and denominator facts (`$denominator`) for the same contexts/units as appropriate and where the fact value is non-zero.
3. If numerator, denominator, or calculated facts are missing, skip that ratio check.
4. Compute the calculated value (`$calcResult`) by dividing the numerator by the denominator and compute the numeric division interval (`division_intervals($numerator, $denominator)`), which returns lower and upper bounds reflecting numeric semantics and decimal tolerances.
5. Compute the reported fact's interval via `fact_intervals($calc)`.
6. Determine overlap: the rule treats the ratio as consistent when intervals overlap (i.e., division lower bound <= reported upper bound and division upper bound >= reported lower bound). If the intervals do not overlap, the rule flags the discrepancy and reports:
   - The ratio concept and its reported fact
   - The numerator and denominator concepts and their values
   - The calculated vs reported intervals
   - Decimals for each component (to help diagnose rounding issues)

## Implementation notes
- The mapping `$NUM_DEN_FULL` in the codebase is used to avoid repeated taxonomy traversal at runtime; in future this mapping may be supplied as metadata.
- The rule uses helper functions `division_intervals` and `fact_intervals` to calculate robust numeric ranges that account for decimals and XULE numeric comparison semantics.
- If numerator or denominator facts are missing or zero, or if the calculated fact is missing, the rule skips that ratio check to avoid false positives.
- For `EarningsPerShareBasic`, the rule skips the check for short `FY`, `Q2`, and `Q3` periods and for filings using the two-class method.
